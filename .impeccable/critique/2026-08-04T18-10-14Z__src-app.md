## Design Specificity Verdict

### LLM assessment

Visually Apoth-specific, but interactionally generic and institutionally fragmented. The retail composition, warm palette, treatment imagery, and repeated `$0 today` language feel authored. The journey does not: it behaves like three products stitched together, with ecommerce checkout, Apoth account onboarding, and an MDI questionnaire. Apoth's strongest differentiator should be a trustworthy, legible handoff between commerce and independent care; the current flow exposes integration vocabulary instead.

### Deterministic scan

The detector returned exactly seven `overused-font` warnings: `src/app/globals.css` lines 34, 71, 83, 113, and 174; and `src/app/weight-loss/page.module.css` lines 20 and 496. These are mostly false positives because adopted `DESIGN.md` explicitly requires Arial/Helvetica for marketing pages. The line 496 result deserves a manual check because the design specification separately calls for Apoth Care in the How It Works display.

### Visual evidence

The live `/weight-loss` page rendered at 1280×720 without horizontal overflow. Its two-column pear product hero, pricing panel, and 44–48px actions were visually composed and consistent with the adopted direction. Mutable browser injection failed because `document.title` was exposed as getter-only, so no reliable user-visible detector overlay was created. Screenshot, DOM facts, and the CLI scan were used as fallback evidence.

## Overall Impression

The visual and compliance foundation is stronger than the funnel logic. The largest opportunity is to replace the two competing sequences with one canonical staged hybrid. The checkout-as-signup route captures a payment method before even the lightweight precheck; the legacy path waits until after clinical approval. The better middle is precheck, account, provider-owned intake, final price and payment authorization, provider review, then explicit treatment activation.

## What's Working

- The weight-loss page discloses both `$99/mo` and `$199/mo`, says medication is included only if prescribed, and repeatedly states that compounded medications are not FDA-approved.
- Checkout makes `$0 today` and the absence of an immediate charge unusually clear.
- The business rules and tests take premature billing, opaque Stripe metadata, signed events, idempotency, and non-retention of clinical answers seriously.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Two contradictory funnels exist

**Why it matters:** Every marketing CTA, including `See if you're eligible`, goes to `/checkout?product=weight`, where users enter payment details before eligibility. A separate `/get-started` path implements privacy notice, precheck, account, consent, intake, review, and deferred billing, but marketing does not link to it. The same patient promise therefore produces different sequences depending on a hidden route choice.

**Fix:** Make one canonical flow: transparent price → privacy notice and minimal precheck → account → independent-portal intake → final price/payment authorization → provider review → explicit treatment selection and activation. Route every marketing CTA to that entry, redirect the obsolete entry, preserve `product=weight`, and label CTAs by the actual next action.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable shape`

### [P1] The payment authorization is too early and commercially incomplete

**Why it matters:** The marketing page shows semaglutide at `$99/mo` and tirzepatide at `$199/mo`, while checkout reduces the offer to one `$99` membership. The checkbox bundles Terms, Privacy, saving a payment method, and a future recurring charge. It does not restate the possible `$199` outcome, the renewal trigger/date, or a treatment-specific final acceptance.

**Fix:** Move SetupIntent collection to after the provider-owned full intake but before review. Show one commercial receipt: `$0 today`; if prescribed, `$99–$199/month` or the exact selected price; what is included; when renewal starts; cancellation/refund terms; prescription not guaranteed. Separate general terms/privacy acknowledgement from recurring-charge authorization. After approval, require explicit treatment and price confirmation before the first charge.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### [P1] The clinical intake boundary and resumability do not match the target architecture

**Why it matters:** The current patient app renders MDI questions inside Apoth, while the adopted product model says the selected white-label portal should own eligibility, intake, drafts, and the clinical record. The legacy questionnaire is one long page, and failure can clear answers. That is a severe abandonment and trust risk on mobile.

**Fix:** Replace `/onboarding/mdi` with an authenticated, short-lived, single-use portal launch. Let the provider portal own question rendering, save/resume, answer review, and clinical help. Apoth should show only handoff status and opaque linkage. Until migration, paginate the legacy questionnaire, show a time estimate, disclose session behavior, and warn before leaving with unsent answers.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable onboard`

### [P2] Progress, responsibility, and timing are inconsistent

**Why it matters:** Checkout shows `Plan / Account / Intake`; the app gate uses `consent / intake / mdi / billing / complete`; the user also encounters verification, provider review, and activation. Patients cannot tell which institution currently has the ball, whether a charge is possible, or how long the next step should take.

**Fix:** Define one shared patient-facing journey model. Every screen should show current step, next step, responsible party, charge status, and expected time. Use patient language such as `Medical intake` and `Provider review`, not `MDI profile`, `case pointer`, or `billing-ready event`.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### [P2] Product context is discarded and invalid choices are presented

**Why it matters:** A visitor arriving from weight loss must choose a care category again, and the precheck offers `Peptides` even though the launch validator rejects it. Repeated selection adds cognitive load and creates an avoidable error path.

**Fix:** Carry the signed/allow-listed product context through precheck and account creation, display it as read-only context, and never render unsupported offerings. Keep only clinician-approved routing questions in the Apoth precheck; if they cross into clinical screening, move them into the provider portal and retain only the result/status.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden`

## Cognitive Load

High: five of eight checklist items fail. Single focus, chunking, one thing at a time, working memory, and progressive disclosure all break across the combined funnel. Grouping, local visual hierarchy, and individual decision-set size are generally controlled.

## Emotional Journey

The weight-loss page begins with confidence and transparent headline pricing. The first deep trust valley is `See if you're eligible` opening a card form. Repeated no-charge language partially repairs that, but the long middle then exposes privacy, account, consent, demographics, MDI, medication disclosure, and billing as separate systems without a stable map or time estimate. Submission brings relief; `Clinical review` is a weak ending because it lacks a response window and a clearly named next owner.

## Persona Red Flags

- **Jordan, first-timer:** Interprets `See if you're eligible` literally, so landing in payment setup feels deceptive. `MDI profile` and `opaque case pointers` are internal terms, not guidance.
- **Casey, distracted mobile user:** The demographic form and full questionnaire are fragile during interruption; progress is not clearly durable and primary actions are not persistently thumb-accessible.
- **Riley, stress tester:** Can enter incompatible flows through `/get-started` and `/checkout`, select an option guaranteed to fail, and expose the `$99` versus `$99–$199` pricing contradiction.

## Minor Observations

- `Clinical profile sex` needs a clearer, provider-approved label and explanation.
- Progress indicators need explicit completed/current semantics, not color alone.
- The marketing page's compounded-drug disclosure is a strength and should remain visible when the flow is consolidated.
- The apparent `font-family: Arial` detector violations are intentional under the adopted design system, not a redesign priority.

## Questions to Consider

- What conversion is worth optimizing if users reach eligibility already suspicious of the CTA?
- Why should a weight-loss visitor choose their care category again?
- Could the portal handoff become Apoth's signature trust moment instead of exposing vendor architecture?
